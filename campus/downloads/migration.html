Cutover checklist

From PRI, H.323, or another SIP trunk

A PRI or CAS trunk speaks channel-associated or ISDN signaling to a gateway. The SIP side of that gateway is a new dialog, not a translation of every Q.931 information element into a header. Treat codec, calling name, and redirecting number as explicit mappings and test them. H.323 is a different protocol family. Interworking belongs on a gateway or call agent that documents the mapping. This campus does not invent that mapping.

A SIP-to-SIP move is easier to test and easier to get wrong quietly. Compare Call-ID behavior across the B2BUA, the From and PAI the carrier will see, Diversion or History-Info, and the SDP address family. Run the old and new trunks in parallel to a pilot range before the cutover window.

RFC 3261 · RFC 7044 History-Info · RFC 3325 PAI · ITU-T H.323

Cutover plan

  • Inventory: numbers, routes, codecs, DTMF, fax mode, recording targets, emergency locations, analog endpoints.
  • Build the new trunk beside the old one. Do not reuse the production peer IP as your only lab.
  • Pilot users for a full business day, including hold, transfer, voicemail, and a queue.
  • Compare one traced call on the old path and the new path. Save both ladders.
  • Freeze changes the day before the window.
  • Cut a route group or dial plan, not every device at once, when the platform allows it.
  • Success checks: inbound, outbound, emergency test number, recording tone or policy, a transfer.
  • Rollback trigger written in advance: error rate, one-way audio, or failed emergency test. Rollback is the old route, already tested.
  • Next business morning: read failures, not just the green test call from the war room.
